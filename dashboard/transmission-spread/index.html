    Transmission & Spread¶

    How did this lineage spread historically, how intense was transmission where the query sits, and is the query part of an ongoing cluster?

    This page reads like a short report in six labelled sections — A through F — moving from the query's place on the tree to a final assessment statement. Every number in it is either observed history or a fitted estimate on observed data; nothing projects the future.

    No forecasts by design

    Earlier versions projected future cases. That was removed: the pipeline doesn't collect enough covariates to project responsibly, so this page reports observed history and fitted estimates only.

    The header — pick a genome, get its dossier¶

    Choose the query in the dropdown — everything below updates. The strip then answers "what do we already know about this genome?":

    • Strain / Clade / Lineage / Outbreak — its family placement (3C.2a1, no named outbreak here).
    • Location — where it was collected (Uganda).
    • Likely origin — the background country its closest relatives point to (USA) — a phylogenetic hint, not travel-history proof.
    • R at sampling — transmission intensity where/when it was collected (R≈3.48; section C shows the uncertainty around it).
    • Cluster = cases — what fraction of the cluster's genomes map to reported cases (17.3%) — a rough genomics-to-epidemiology bridge.

    A. Genomic relationship & how it spread — observed history¶

    The tree shows the query (red star) among its closest sampled relatives; below it, the lineage's movement summary:

    • Spread rate — 1971.1 km/yr: how fast this lineage moved geographically across its sampled history.
    • Max extent / Countries / Origin — 15126 km span, 3 countries, origin Australia (where the deepest sampled ancestor sits).

    Read 'origin' cautiously

    Origin and spread rate are computed from sampled genomes — they describe where the sequenced record starts, which can lag or miss the true source country entirely.

    B. Closest relatives — outbreak impact over time — observed history¶

    For the countries where the query's closest relatives circulated, the panels show monthly reported cases (left) and monthly deaths (right) — the real-world footprint of the lineage your genome belongs to.

    In the H3N2 example, cases show clear seasonal peaks through 2016–2026 while deaths stay essentially flat — a high-circulation, low-severity profile typical of seasonal influenza rather than, say, an Ebola signature. This is the epidemiological counterpart to the genetic tree.

    C. Transmission intensity — estimated R(t)¶

    The R(t) curve plots the time-varying reproduction number in the query's location, with the dashed line marking when the query was sampled:

    • At sampling, R = 3.48 (95% CrI 0.70–8.38) — the credible interval is the honest part: with sparse data it spans "under control" to "growing fast", so the dashboard concludes stable or uncertain rather than pretending precision.
    • Shaded band = 95% credible interval; spikes earlier in the series are past transmission surges at that location.

    R(t) is a location estimate, not a genome property

    R(t) describes transmission in that place at that time, estimated from reported cases — it isn't a property of this virus specifically, and wide intervals mean "we can't tell", not "we can't be bothered".

    D. Genetic transmission cluster — tree-derived¶

    The orange callout says it directly:

    Query is genetically distinct — no background genome falls within the transmission-cluster threshold, so it likely represents a separate introduction or an unsampled lineage.

    "Cluster = cases" in the header and this section are the same idea at different scales: does the query sit inside a sampled transmission cluster? Here the answer is no — which usually means either a separate introduction or, very commonly, that the actual cluster simply wasn't sequenced.

    E. How did it behave historically? — observed history¶

    Compare the query's strain (and optionally others) on its track record:

    • 3C.2a1: 306.75 linked cases, 0 linked deaths, 0% mean CFR, 3 countries, 4 years active, behaviour = persistent, multi-country.

    This is the strain's resume — the counterfactual question it answers is "what has this lineage done before?", which is the strongest observed evidence for what it might do again.

    F. Transmission & Spread Assessment¶

    The final card writes the conclusions in two clearly-labelled voices:

    • OBSERVED — what the historical record shows: "linked to 306 cases across three countries … persisted for four years without recorded deaths … likely originated in the USA based on its genetic characteristics."
    • MODEL-DERIVED — what the fitted estimates add: "R ≈ 3.48 with a wide range of 0.7–8.4 … growth phase stable or uncertain … slow doubling time of about 38 days … sparse weekly cases, so reliability may be limited."

    The footer states the rule the whole pipeline follows: Observed = historical evidence. Model-derived = growth-model fit to observed data. The AI-generated tag (qwen2.5:14b) marks the prose; the numbers are the pipeline's.

    Limitations

    • R(t) and spread rate inherit every gap in case reporting and sequencing density — wide credible intervals are the norm in sparse settings.
    • Origin / likely origin describe the sampled record; undersampled regions routinely misattribute it.
    • "Genetically distinct" ≠ "new lineage" — it most often means the real cluster wasn't sequenced.
    • The assessment card is a summary; the numbers in sections A–E are the evidence to cite.

    What to watch for¶

    1. R(t) with a credible interval clearly above 1 at sampling — this example is ambiguous, a sustained R > 1 with tight bounds is not.
    2. Query inside a defined cluster with a short branch to a named outbreak — the closest thing to "ongoing transmission" genomics can show.
    3. A strain resume that disagrees with the badge — e.g. MONITOR next to a lineage with documented high-CFR history would deserve a second look.
